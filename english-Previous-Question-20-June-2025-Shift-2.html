Reading Comprehension Passage (Questions 61–65)

Over the years, the United Nations has played a crucial role in resolving numerous conflicts, often through the actions of the Security Council, the body primarily responsible, under the United Nations Charter, for maintaining international peace and security. When the Council receives a complaint about a potential threat to peace, its first step is to encourage the parties to reach a peaceful agreement. In some cases, the Council itself conducts investigations and facilitates mediation. It may appoint special representatives or request the Secretary-General to do so, or to offer his good offices. The Council may also propose principles for a peaceful resolution. When a dispute escalates into violence, the Council's main priority is to end the conflict as quickly as possible. The Council has frequently issued ceasefire directives that have helped prevent large-scale hostilities. It also deploys UN peacekeeping missions to reduce tensions, separate opposing forces, and establish the conditions necessary for sustainable peace once settlements are reached. The Council may also impose enforcement measures, such as economic sanctions like trade embargoes or collective military action. The United Nations Security Council is composed of 15 member states, which include 5 permanent members China, France, Russia, the United Kingdom, and the United States of America and 10 non-permanent members elected for two-year term by the General Assembly. The permanent members hold veto power, giving them significant influence over the Council's decisions, while the non-permanent members contribute to maintaining international peace and security through their rotating representation.
