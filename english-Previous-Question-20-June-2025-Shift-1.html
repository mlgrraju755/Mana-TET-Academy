The effects of climate change on water resources can have profound consequences for both our environment and our daily lives. As global temperatures rise, the patterns of precipitation—such as when, where, and how much rain falls—are becoming increasingly unpredictable. Some regions are experiencing more intense and frequent rainfall, while others are facing prolonged droughts. These shifts in precipitation patterns pose significant challenges to water management and accessibility worldwide. Flooding is emerging as a growing concern due to the changing climate. Compared to the early 20th century, precipitation events have become more intense, heavier, and more frequent in many parts of the world. As a result, the risk of flooding has increased, especially in areas already vulnerable to such events. This trend is expected to worsen as climate change continues, highlighting the need for better flood prevention strategies and infrastructure. At the same time, droughts are becoming more prevalent, particularly as water usage escalates during hotter weather, with agriculture being a major contributor. Just as we sweat more in extreme heat, higher temperatures cause plants to lose, or transpire, more water, prompting farmers to use even more water to irrigate their crops. Snowpack, which is essential for providing fresh water to many regions, is also being affected. As snow levels decrease and snowmelt occurs earlier, it is becoming an increasingly unreliable source of water, especially in areas where rainfall is limited during warmer months.
